APriori:
-- A master private key, dA, and its public key, PA
-- The number of raffle tickets in our Merkle Tree, M
-- The catalog of prizes, of size N
-- A sequence of M unique private keys, dBi
-- A sequence of M unique public keys, PBi
-- A Merkle Tree of M Diffie-Hellman Keys, Si = dA*PBi
-- Merkle Tree root R
-- A bloom filter, B, built from the PBi to ensure uniqueness

Using secure resources:
1)  Shuffles the indices 1 through M and keep only the top N
2)  Shuffle the indices 1 through N 
3)  Store the (i, k) winner pairs by zipping (1) and (2) with top security
4)  Retrieve PBi for each k to publish the labels for each prize publicly.
5)  Retrieve dBi, Si, and PathSi to prove each prize is in the Raffle Ticket Merkle tree, and 
    that we know its location and the reveal key that exposes the winner once published.
    -- Si is already in the published Merkle Tree, and anyone can calculate or verify its Commitment
       Proof Path.  This holds for every other S committed to the Merkle Tree.  What is secret here
       is which prize a particular Si with proof path PathSi has been selected to receive
    -- To cheat, we would have to find some other dB that combines with PA to produce one of the
       other raffle ticket S values _and_ multiplies with G to yield the same Prize ID.  Our raffle
       can be trusted because that is a computationally imparactical problem to try solving.
6)  For each (PA, PBi, Si, R), prove knowledge about witness (dBi, i, PathSi)
    -- R is the Merkle Root for a commitment of Si with proof path PathSi
    -- dB*PA = S
    -- dB*G = PB
    -- [PathSi] <-relTo-> (R) <-relTo-> [Si] <-relTo-> (PA)        [dBi] <-relTo-> (PBi) 
                                         |                           |
                                         \-----------relTo-----------/
       -- [] are proven known but not revealed
       -- () are public givens
   -- Each proof publicly uses the Master public key (PA), one specific prize public key, and the
      Merkle Root for all raffle tickets.
   -- Each proves that a winning raffle ticket for the referenced prize is committed in the Merkle
      tree, that we know which ticket it is, and that we know a private key that will expose the
      winning ticket.  These facts are proving without revealing their values, but the nature of 
      the proof shows it would be computationally infeasible for us to cheat by trying to change
      the winning ticket after the fact.
   -- An accompanying smart contract is required that guarantees procedural aspects:
      -- All raffle tickets are treated equally until the game ends
      -- Sets a time frame for the game to end
      -- Requires the operator to either reveal and distribute prizes or refund ticket holders and 
         suffer an self-imposed penalty if prizes are withheld.
 
3)  This series of proofs establishes:
-- Every k from N is represented by an unrevealed PB that is somewhere in the ticket sequence,
   and PA 
-- (dA, dB, i) as the reveal tuple for each prize, K
